Nexora ERP: User Manual & Reference

Nexora is an ERP for service companies (agencies, consultancies, studios, IT services). It follows the whole chain of the business: people → clients → projects → hours → invoices → cash → profit. This document describes what has been delivered, how each module behaves, the business rules behind it, how the code is organised, and what remains to be built. It follows the Nexora ERP cahier des charges.

Next.js 16 · React 19 · TypeScript Tailwind CSS v4 · Nexora DS 9 languages incl. Arabic (RTL) 398 unit tests · Playwright e2e Demo data in the browser (backend = Phase 7)

01Welcome to Nexora

Nexora is the operating system of a service company. It connects the people who do the work, the clients who pay for it, the projects it belongs to and the hours spent on them, and turns all of that into invoices, cash and profit figures – without spreadsheets in between.

Who it is for

Agencies, consultancies, design and video studios, IT service companies, engineering offices: any team that sells time, projects or monthly retainers.

What it replaces

The timesheet spreadsheet, the separate invoicing tool, the HR file, the 'who owes us money' list and the end-of-month profitability workbook.

What you get

One place where every hour is approved, billed and measured, with the rules of your company (rates, approvals, taxes, numbering) applied automatically.

02Signing in and the demo

  1. Open Nexora in your browser (locally: http://localhost:3000).
  2. On the sign-in page, enter your work email and password, or click Instant Demo Preview to explore with sample companies.
  3. You land on Dashboard → Analytics. Your name and photo are in the top-right corner; click them for your profile, security settings and sign out.

04Roles and what each person sees

Every member of a workspace has a role. The role decides which pages and buttons appear. Sensitive information such as hourly costs and margins is hidden from people who do not need it.

Rule: nobody can approve their own hours, leave or expenses – the button is not offered and the system refuses it.

05First-time setup (checklist)

Do these steps once, in this order. Allow about 30 minutes.

  1. Company identity – System → Workspace settings → Company: legal name, ICE, tax ID, trade register, address, base currency, fiscal-year start, time zone, invoice number format. These appear on every invoice.
  2. Lists – Settings → Lists: create your departments, the year's public holidays, leave types (annual, sick, unpaid…), expense categories and task labels.
  3. Approval rules – Settings → Approvals: who approves timesheets, leave and expenses (the person's manager, the project manager, or a named person), in one or two steps.
  4. Team – Organization → Employees → Add employee: role, department, manager, working days, weekly capacity, hourly cost and billable rate.
  5. Clients – Organization → Clients → Add client: legal details, billing address, currency, invoice language, payment terms, contacts, and any special rates (rate card).
  6. Projects – Organization → Projects → New project: client, manager, team, dates, budget type and amount, milestones.
  7. Tell the team to log their hours every day and submit their week on Friday.
Set your invoice number format and fiscal year before issuing the first invoice. Numbers are given in sequence per fiscal year and can never be reused.

06Employee guide: your day and your week

Every day (2 minutes)

  1. Start the timer when you begin a task (Organization → Timesheets → timer bar), choose the project and task, and stop it when you switch. Or use Quick log to enter hours afterwards.
  2. Open Projects to see your tasks; update a task’s status when it changes; answer comments where someone @mentioned you.

Every Friday

  1. Open Timesheets and check the week: each row is a project/task, each column a day. The bar at the top shows your total against your expected hours.
  2. Fill any gaps (type decimal hours such as 1.5; hours are rounded to the nearest 15 minutes).
  3. Click Submit week. The hours are locked and go to your approver.
  4. If hours come back sent back, read the reason, correct them and submit again.

Leave and expenses

  • Planning → Leave → Request leave: type, dates, note. Approved leave reduces your expected hours automatically.
  • Finance → Expenses → Add expense: date, category, project (if any), amount, receipt, and whether it should be re-billed to the client.
Tip: Copy last week fills the timesheet with last week's rows (without hours) so you only type the numbers.

07Manager guide: keep projects on track

Weekly routine

  1. Monday – plan: Planning → Workload shows each person's planned and logged hours against capacity. Rebalance tasks for anyone above 100%.
  2. During the week – follow: in each project, the board shows work in progress; health turns At risk or Off track when dates or budget slip. Budget alerts appear at 80% and 100%.
  3. Friday/Monday – approve: Organization → Time approvals shows one card per person and week. Check the hours per project, then Approve or Send back with a reason.
  4. Leave and expenses: approve them from Planning → Leave and Finance → Expenses.

Running a project

  • Break the work into milestones and tasks with estimates, assignees, due dates and labels.
  • Use filters (assignee, label, priority) and Save view to keep your favourite filters.
  • If the automatic health does not reflect reality, use Override health and give a reason – it is shown to everyone and recorded.
  • Use comments with @Name to ask a teammate something on the task itself; the history keeps every change.
Approved hours are what the client will be billed and what costs are measured on. Approve carefully: once invoiced they can no longer be reopened.

08Accountant guide: invoicing and month-end

Month-end routine

  1. Check that all hours of the month are approved (Time approvals should be empty).
  2. Finance → Client invoices → Ready to bill lists, per client, the approved hours not yet invoiced. Click Create invoice.
  3. For fixed-price, milestone, retainer, deposit or free invoices use New invoice and pick the type.
  4. Review the draft (lines, tax rate, due date), then Issue – the invoice receives its number – and Send.
  5. When money arrives, open the invoice and Record payment (date, amount, method, reference). Partial payments are allowed.
  6. Mistake on an issued invoice? Create a credit note; never delete.
  7. Export invoices and payments as CSV for your accounting software.
  8. Lock the month: Settings → Company → Period lock to the last day of the month.

Following up on cash

Finance → Receivables shows who owes what, split by how late it is. Start with the right-hand columns (61–90 and over 90 days).

09Director guide: reading the business

  1. Open Dashboard → Analytics.
  2. Pick a period (last 30 days, 90 days, 12 months or year to date) and a comparison (previous period or same period last year).
  3. Read the four cards: revenue earned, gross margin, team utilization, cash collected.
  4. Use the revenue bridge to see where growth or decline came from (existing clients, new clients, lost clients), and the Clients at risk tab for overdue money and sharp drops.
  5. Filter by client or team to drill down; click Save as report for a PDF or CSV.
  6. Ask Victor (the assistant button) questions such as Which clients are late paying?

12Clients

Where: Organization → Clients.

  1. Click Add client.
  2. Enter the name, industry, status (lead, active, archived) and account manager.
  3. Fill the legal details: legal name, ICE, tax ID, billing address.
  4. Choose currency, invoice language and payment terms (days).
  5. Add contacts and mark the primary one.
  6. Optional: add a rate card – a special rate for a person or for a job title for this client.
  7. Save.

Which rate is used?

When hours are billed, Nexora looks for a rate in this order and uses the first it finds:

  1. This person on the client's rate card
  2. This person's job title on the client's rate card
  3. The client's default hourly rate
  4. The employee's billable rate valid on the day of the work
A client with invoices cannot be deleted – archive it instead. Archived clients disappear from pickers but keep their history.

Rules that protect your data

  • Hours are rounded to 15 minutes; at most 24 hours per day.
  • Only members of a project can log time on it; finished or cancelled projects refuse new hours.

Create an invoice from hours

  1. In Ready to bill, click Create invoice on the client.
  2. Select the hours to include and how to group the lines: by person, by task or by day.
  3. Choose the tax rate and issue date; the due date follows the client's payment terms.
  4. Deposits already paid by the client are offered for deduction.
  5. Create – you get a draft you can still edit.

Other invoice types (New invoice)

TypeHow to use it
Fixed shareEnter a percentage of a fixed-price project; Nexora refuses to bill more than the price
MilestoneChoose a milestone and the amount due
RetainerChoose the month; the monthly amount is billed, plus hours beyond the included ones at the overage rate
DepositAn advance before the work; deducted on the final invoice
FreeAny lines you type

From draft to paid

  1. Draft – edit lines, taxes (one rate per line if needed), withholding, notes.
  2. Issue – the invoice receives its number and is locked.
  3. Send – recorded with date and recipient.
  4. Record payment – one or several payments; the balance updates.
  5. Paid – automatically when the balance reaches zero.

Correcting an invoice

  • A draft can be edited or deleted freely (it has no number yet).
  • An issued invoice is never deleted. Create a credit note (full or partial). Credit notes have their own numbering (CN-…) and can free the hours so they can be billed again.
  • An invoice with no payments can be voided; one with payments must be credited.

Currencies and taxes

Each invoice uses the client's currency; an exchange rate converts it to your base currency for reports. Several VAT rates can appear on one invoice (a tax line per rate), and a withholding tax can be deducted from the total.

24How figures are calculated

Cost of hoursAll logged hours × the person's hourly cost on that date
Gross margin(Revenue − cost of hours − approved expenses) ÷ revenue
Available hoursWorking days × hours per day, minus approved leave and holidays
UtilizationBillable hours ÷ available hours (target 75%)

Balance dueInvoice total − payments − credit notes

25Frequently asked questions

Why can't I edit my hours?

They are submitted, approved or invoiced, or the day is in a locked period. Ask your manager to send them back or reopen them.

Why does an invoice have no number?

It is still a draft. The number is given when you click Issue, so deleted drafts never leave gaps.

How do I cancel an invoice I already sent?

Create a credit note from the invoice. If no payment was recorded you can also void it.

A client paid only part of the invoice.

Record the payment with the amount received; the invoice shows Partly paid and the balance due.

A rate changed – will old invoices change?

No. Approved hours keep the rate frozen at approval, and issued invoices never change.

Why is someone's utilization low?

Hours not logged, non-billable work, or leave. Check Team capacity on Analytics and the person's timesheet.

Can a client see Nexora?

Not yet; the client portal is planned for a later release.

Why don't my colleagues see my data?

In this version data is stored in your browser only. Shared data arrives with the server version.

30Quality and security

  • Every change is checked automatically: type check, lint, translations, 398 unit tests, production build, end-to-end tests, dependency audit, and a guard that blocks demo mode in production.
  • Spreadsheet exports neutralise formulas; PDFs and reports escape all text; network requests time out.
  • Current limits: permissions are applied in the screens only and data lives in the browser. The server version enforces permissions on the server and stores data securely with backups.
